Going solar is not one decision. It is several decisions stacked together: system size, equipment, financing, installer quality, and future compatibility.

Here is the process we recommend:

1. Learn the baseline
Start with the installation guide so you understand how panels, inverters, batteries, permits, and interconnection fit together. You do not need to become an expert. You do need a working map of the process.

2. Translate the quote
Installer proposals often hide important differences behind similar monthly payments. We help you compare equipment, estimated production, warranties, financing terms, and what is actually included in the install.

3. Pressure-test the installer
A good system is only as good as the company behind it. We encourage homeowners to look beyond polished sales materials and think about service history, longevity, and whether the installer is a fit for a long-life asset.

4. Plan beyond the sale
Solar is increasingly part of a broader home energy setup. If you expect to add an EV, battery storage, or a smart electrical panel, that should shape the decision now, not later.

5. Make the choice
Once the proposal is legible, the installer is credible, and the system fits your longer-term plan, the decision gets easier. You are not guessing. You are choosing.

This site is built to support that sequence. Each resource is designed to move you from broad research to a narrower, more confident answer.

If you are early, read the guides. If you have quotes, use the comparison content. If you are close to signing, make sure the deal still works for the next 10 years, not just the next 10 months.
