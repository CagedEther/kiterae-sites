If you are close to signing, the installer is one of the biggest risk factors in the entire solar purchase. Panels, batteries, and financing all matter, but the company that designs, installs, and supports the system will shape your experience for years.

That is why the best solar installer is not simply the one with the lowest price. It is the one that gives you confidence in the work, the support, and the long-term stability behind the contract.

Use this checklist to evaluate your options:

### 1. Compare what is actually being sold
Make sure the quotes use the same assumptions for equipment, system size, expected production, and warranty coverage. If one proposal is based on different hardware or a more optimistic estimate, the comparison is not clean.

### 2. Ask about service after installation
A solar system can last for decades. Ask who handles troubleshooting, monitoring, warranty support, and repair coordination if something fails later.

### 3. Check company stability
A common homeowner fear is ending up with an unsupported system if the installer disappears. You do not need perfect certainty, but you should ask direct questions about business history, service model, and how warranty claims are handled.

### 4. Understand the financing tie-in
Some installers push a preferred lender or package the numbers in a way that obscures the true cost. Review the financing separately from the hardware so you can judge the proposal on its merits.

### 5. Think beyond the first year
The right installer should help you plan for future electrification needs, not act as if the project ends at commissioning. If you may add an EV charger, battery, or smart panel later, that should be part of the conversation now.

The homeowner’s job is not to become a solar engineer. It is to ask enough of the right questions to avoid regret.

If you already have 2 or 3 quotes, the best next step is to slow down, normalize the proposals, and compare the companies behind them—not just the price on page one.
