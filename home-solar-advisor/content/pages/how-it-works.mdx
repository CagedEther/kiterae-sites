Home solar gets complicated because each part of the purchase is bundled together: equipment, design, financing, installer reputation, utility assumptions, incentives, and timing. Our job is to separate those layers so you can evaluate them on their own merits.

Here is the process.

1. **Start with your goals**  
   We begin with the practical reason you are considering solar: lower bills, backup power, better resilience, or a longer-term electrification plan. That context matters because the best system for a cost-focused buyer is not always the best system for a resilience-focused buyer.

2. **Collect the documents**  
   If you have proposals, we use them as the starting point. If you are still early, we help you understand what information matters before quotes arrive: roof condition, utility rate structure, battery intent, and future EV plans.

3. **Normalize the comparison**  
   Quotes rarely use the same assumptions. We help you compare the things that actually affect value: system size, equipment quality, financing terms, estimated production, warranty coverage, and service expectations.

4. **Check the installer risk**  
   A solar system is a long-lived asset. That makes installer stability, support model, and warranty handling part of the decision, not an afterthought. We encourage homeowners to ask about who will service the system if something goes wrong years later.

5. **Plan beyond year one**  
   The purchase should make sense with future home upgrades, especially EV charging, storage, and smart electrical panels. We treat solar as part of a home energy roadmap, not a one-time transaction.

6. **Make the next move with confidence**  
   Once the tradeoffs are clear, the decision gets simpler. You can choose the option that fits your home, your budget, and your future plans instead of the one with the flashiest pitch.

This is not about slowing you down. It is about removing avoidable regret.
